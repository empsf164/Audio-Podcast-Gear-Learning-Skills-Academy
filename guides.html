GUIDE 01 • TRANSDUCER SELECTION

Which Microphone Is Right for Your Podcast?

Most creators make the mistake of buying sensitive condenser microphones in echoey living rooms. A microphone does not know what sounds you want and what sounds you don’t—it simply transduces air pressure fluctuations into electrical voltage.

Dynamic Microphones

Heavier moving coil diaphragm. Requires close speaking distance (2–4 inches). Naturally rejects distant HVAC, fan rumble, and room echo.

Condenser Microphones

Ultra-thin gold-sputtered foil. Captures delicate top-end shimmer and mouth nuances. Requires a treated acoustic room or vocal booth.

GUIDE 02 • SIGNAL TOPOLOGY

USB vs XLR: Which Should You Choose?

USB microphones combine the capsule, preamp, analog-to-digital converter (ADC), and headphone amplifier onto a single internal chip. XLR microphones output raw balanced analog voltage, giving you the freedom to choose your interface, preamp, and multi-mic expansions.

Feature USB-C Connection XLR Analog Connection
Setup Complexity Plug-and-play into PC/Mac/iPad Requires Audio Interface or Mixer
Multi-Mic Recording Difficult without aggregate software Effortless (2, 4, or 8 mics into interface)
Cable Length Limited to 3 meters before signal drop Up to 100 meters with zero interference

GUIDE 03 • PHYSICAL ERGONOMICS

How to Build an Ergonomic Podcast Setup

Good posture directly translates into consistent vocal resonance. By mounting your microphone on a low-profile boom arm, you eliminate mechanical desk vibrations caused by typing and mouse movement while keeping eye contact clear with your camera and co-host.

  • The 3:1 Rule: Maintain a 3-to-1 distance ratio between adjacent microphones to prevent hollow phase comb filtering.
  • Shockmount Isolation: Decouple your mic from desk rumble caused by elbow leaning and table thumps.
  • Direct Sidetone Monitoring: Always monitor your voice with closed-back headphones to avoid shouting.

REFERENCE DICTIONARY

The Audio Engineer's Glossary

EIN (Equivalent Input Noise)

A measure of the electrical noise created internally by a preamp circuit, usually expressed in dBu. Lower numbers (such as -129 dBu) mean virtually silent gain with zero perceptible hiss.

LUFS (Loudness Units Full Scale)

The international broadcast standard for perceived loudness. Podcast platforms normalize speech between -16 LUFS (Apple/Stereo) and -19 LUFS (Mono).

Proximity Effect

An increase in low-frequency bass response that occurs when a sound source gets closer to a directional (cardioid) microphone. Speaking within 2 inches yields radio-DJ warmth.

Polar Null Point

The exact physical angle where a microphone is least sensitive. For cardioid patterns, the null point is 180° directly behind the capsule; pointing this at your computer fan shields the recording.

Comb Filtering

An acoustic interference hollow artifact caused when the exact same voice reaches a microphone at two different times (e.g. directly and reflecting off an untreated bare wall).

+48V Phantom Power

Direct current electrical voltage delivered through balanced XLR pins 2 and 3 to polarize true condenser microphone capsules and power internal converters.
